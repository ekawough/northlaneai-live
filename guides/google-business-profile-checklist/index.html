Google Business Profile checklist for local businesses (2026)

By Ethan, NorthLane AIUpdated 2026-11-21

Quick answer

A complete Google Business Profile has four parts: accurate basics (name, category, address or service area, phone, hours), what you sell (every service or product, with a plain description), proof (recent photos and steady reviews with replies) and upkeep (updates, answered questions, holiday hours). It's free, and for most local businesses it's the first thing a customer sees, before the website.

The basics: is it accurate?

  1. Your business name matches your sign and your paperwork. No extra keywords added.
  2. Your primary category is the closest match to your main work, and you've added the secondary categories that apply.
  3. Your address is right, or your service area is set if you go to the customer.
  4. Your phone number is the one you actually answer.
  5. Your hours are right, including holidays and any emergency hours.
  6. Your website link goes to a working page.

What you sell: is everything listed?

  1. Every service or product you want more of is listed, each with a short plain description.
  2. Your business description says what you do, who for, and where, in the first sentence.
  3. The attributes that apply are turned on, like appointment only, wheelchair access or languages spoken.
  4. If you take bookings, the booking or appointment link is filled in.

Proof: does it look alive?

  1. You have recent photos of real work, your team, your place or your truck. Not stock photos.
  2. You ask every customer for a review with a direct link.
  3. You reply to every review, good and bad.

Upkeep: is someone watching it?

  1. You post an update at least once a month: a finished job, an offer, a seasonal reminder.
  2. You check for customer questions and suggested edits, because anyone can suggest a change to your listing.
  3. Once a month you look at the calls, direction requests and website clicks Google reports for your profile.

Then match it everywhere else

Copy the exact name, address, phone and hours to Yelp, Apple Maps, Bing Places and Facebook. When the details match everywhere, Google and AI tools can trust they're talking about the same business.

FAQ

Is a Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Creating, verifying and managing the profile costs nothing. You only pay if you choose to run Google Ads.

I work out of my truck and have no storefront. Can I still have one?

Yes. Set it up as a service-area business: you hide your address and list the cities or areas you serve.

How often should I update it?

Check it monthly, and right away when hours, phone or services change. A profile with wrong hours loses calls.
