How to get more Google reviews without breaking the rules

By Ethan, NorthLane AIUpdated 2026-11-21

Quick answer

The way to get more Google reviews is to ask every customer, right after the job or visit, with a direct link. Google allows asking and encourages it. What it bans is paying or rewarding people for reviews, writing fake ones, and asking only the customers you think are happy. A steady trickle of honest reviews beats one big burst.

What Google allows and what it bans

  • Allowed: asking any customer for a review, in person, by text, by email or on a card.
  • Allowed: sending a direct link or QR code so it takes one tap.
  • Banned: paying for reviews, or offering discounts, gifts or contest entries in exchange for them.
  • Banned: reviews from you, your staff or anyone who wasn't a customer.
  • Banned: asking only happy customers, or steering unhappy ones away from leaving a review. Google calls this review gating.

Breaking these can get reviews removed or a profile restricted. Read Google's own rules on prohibited and restricted content before you try anything clever.

A simple system that works

  1. Get your review link. In your Google Business Profile, choose the option to ask for reviews and copy the link. Turn it into a QR code too.
  2. Ask at the right moment. Right after the job is done or the meal is paid for, while the customer is still pleased.
  3. Ask out loud first. “Would you mind leaving us a Google review? I'll text you the link.” People do more for a person than for an automated message.
  4. Send the link the same day. A short text or email with nothing but a thank-you and the link.
  5. Make it routine. Every customer, every time. Customer software like GoHighLevel can send the request automatically when a job is marked done.

What to say

Keep it short and don't tell people what to write. Something like: “Thanks for choosing us today. If you have a minute, a Google review really helps a small business like ours.” Then the link.

Reply to every review

Thank people for good reviews in a sentence or two. For a bad one, stay calm, say what you'll do about it, and offer a way to reach you directly. Future customers read your replies as much as the reviews.

What not to worry about

A few less-than-perfect reviews are normal. A fair complaint with a calm reply often helps more than it hurts.

FAQ

Can I offer a discount for a review?

No. Google bans offering money, discounts or gifts in exchange for reviews, even if you don't ask for a good one.

Can I get a bad review removed?

Only Google can remove it, and only if it breaks their rules, like spam or a review meant for a different business. You can flag those. For a fair bad review, a calm reply is the best move.

How many reviews do I need?

There's no magic number. In BrightLocal's 2026 research about half of consumers said they wouldn't use a business with fewer than 20, and recent reviews matter more than old ones.
