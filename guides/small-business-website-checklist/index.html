What a small-business website needs in 2026

By Ethan, NorthLane AIUpdated 2026-11-21

Quick answer

A small-business website needs to do three jobs: say what you do and where in the first screen, make it easy to call, book or buy from any page, and be readable by Google and AI tools. That means one page per service, real photos, real reviews, fast loading on a phone, and structured data. It does not need animations, a blog you won't update, or a dozen pages nobody visits.

The first screen

  • A headline that says what you do, for whom, and where.
  • One main action: call, book or get a quote. Not four buttons.
  • Your phone number as a tap-to-call link on phones.
  • A real photo of you, your team, your work or your place.

The pages you need

  • A page for each service. One “Services” page listing everything is hard to rank and hard for AI tools to quote.
  • A page for each main area you serve, if you travel to customers.
  • An about page with a real person on it. People buy from the owner, especially locally.
  • A contact page with phone, email, hours and the area you cover.
  • Privacy policy and terms, in plain English.

Proof

  • Real reviews, with where they came from. Never invented ones.
  • Photos of finished work.
  • Licenses, insurance and memberships, if your trade has them.
  • Clear pricing, or at least how pricing works. In BrightLocal's 2026 research, clear pricing was one of the top things people look for.

Speed and phones

  • Pages that load in about two seconds on a phone. Test yours free at PageSpeed Insights.
  • Text you can read and buttons you can tap without zooming.
  • Content in the page itself, not loaded later by scripts, so search engines and AI tools can read it.

What machines read

  • A clear title and description on every page.
  • Schema markup (hidden labels that tell Google and AI tools what's on the page) for your business, services and FAQs.
  • A sitemap submitted to Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
  • A robots.txt file (it tells search and AI tools what they may read) that doesn't block AI crawlers like GPTBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot.
  • The same name, address and phone as your Google Business Profile.

Knowing what works

  • Every form and call button tracked, so you know which page a lead came from.
  • A form that actually reaches you, tested once a month.
  • A fast reply to every lead. The quickest business to answer usually gets the job.

What you can skip

Sliders, pop-ups, scrolling banners, stock photos of strangers shaking hands, and a blog nobody keeps up. A plain site that loads fast and answers the customer's question beats a fancy one that doesn't.

FAQ

How many pages does a small-business website need?

Usually a home page, one page per service, an about page, a contact page, and privacy and terms. Add a page per service area if you travel to customers. That's often 8 to 15 pages.

Do I need a blog?

Only if you'll keep it up. A few solid guides that answer real customer questions do more than a blog with two posts from last year.

Is a Facebook page or Google profile enough?

They're a start, and the Google profile matters a lot. But a website is the one place you control, and it's where Google and AI tools look for the details about what you do.
